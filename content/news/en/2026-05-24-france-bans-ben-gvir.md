# France Bans Israeli Security Minister Ben Gvir Over Activist Treatment

**France has announced a ban on Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir from entering French territory**, following widespread international condemnation of his actions toward foreign activists aboard a Gaza-bound humanitarian flotilla. The decision, announced on Saturday, marks an escalation in diplomatic tensions and represents one of Europe's strongest responses to the incident.

French Foreign Minister **Jean-Noel Barrot** declared the ban on X (formerly Twitter), stating that Ben Gvir is prohibited from entering France effective immediately due to "his reprehensible actions towards French and European citizens" who were part of the flotilla operation. Barrot also announced that France, alongside Italy, is calling for European Union-level sanctions against the far-right Israeli minister.

## The Incident and Global Outcry

The ban follows a global wave of criticism triggered when Ben Gvir published a video on Wednesday showing the treatment of foreign activists detained by Israeli forces. The video, captioned "Welcome to Israel," depicted dozens of activists forced to kneel with their foreheads pressed to the ground and their hands bound. In the footage, Ben Gvir can be seen heckling the detained activists while waving an Israeli flag.

The activists had been seized in international waters by Israeli soldiers after attempting to breach Israel's blockade of Gaza as part of the Global Sumud Flotilla. According to [France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260523-france-bans-israeli-security-minister-ben-gvir-from-country), the flotilla represented the latest attempt by activists to deliver humanitarian aid to Gaza following the interception of a previous convoy the previous month.

The video sparked immediate international condemnation, with multiple nations and officials criticizing both the treatment of the activists and Ben Gvir's decision to publicize the incident. Following the outcry, Israel announced it would deport the detained activists.

## France's Position and European Response

While Foreign Minister Barrot acknowledged that France does not support the flotilla's actions, arguing they serve "no useful purpose," he emphasized that the nation "cannot tolerate French nationals being threatened, intimidated or brutalised in this way, especially by a public official."

Approximately 36 French nationals were aboard the flotilla, making France one of the countries with the largest contingent of affected citizens. This significant French presence appears to have strengthened the government's response to the incident.

France is not alone in its reaction. **Spain has also urged the European Union to impose sanctions against Ben Gvir**, while the United Kingdom summoned Israeli officials in response to the incident. The coordinated European response suggests growing diplomatic pressure on Israel regarding the treatment of foreign activists and humanitarian workers.

## What This Means for Visitors

The ban on Ben Gvir does not affect ordinary travelers or tourism to France. Entry restrictions for foreign officials are diplomatic measures that do not impact visa policies or travel procedures for citizens of other nations. Visitors from Israel and other countries continue to enter France under normal visa and entry requirements.

However, the incident underscores ongoing tensions in the Middle East and Europe's increasingly vocal stance on humanitarian concerns related to the Gaza situation. The diplomatic friction may influence broader EU-Israel relations in the coming months.

## What's Next

France and Italy's call for EU-level sanctions against Ben Gvir will likely be discussed at upcoming European Union meetings. The success of such measures would depend on consensus among all EU member states. Meanwhile, the incident has added another layer to the complex diplomatic relationship between Europe and Israel, particularly regarding humanitarian access and the treatment of foreign nationals in conflict zones.
