Kenya is set to host the inaugural Africa Forward 2026 summit outside a Francophone country in partnership with **France**, drawing African leaders and global business executives to Nairobi on May 11, 2026. This event underscores President **William Ruto**'s push for Western alliances since taking office, positioning Kenya as East Africa's stable hub, but it has ignited debates over neo-colonial influences given France's fraught history in Africa.

### Background on the France-Kenya Ties

France's engagement with Kenya marks a strategic pivot as Paris grapples with diminishing sway in its former West African colonies. President **Emmanuel Macron** has sought new footholds, turning to Kenya's openness to European investment amid regional instability. According to **Al Jazeera**, this partnership builds on Kenya's reputation for political stability and economic accessibility, contrasting with France's recent setbacks in the Sahel region, where military withdrawals have eroded influence.

The summit itself represents a milestone, being the first such gathering hosted by a non-Francophone nation. It aims to foster continental dialogue on development, trade, and security, with France positioning itself as a key player. However, domestic and international critics argue that Kenya risks subordinating its interests to a power with a colonial legacy. Macron's January 2025 remarks to French ambassadors—that Sahel countries "would not be sovereign today" without French military intervention—have fueled accusations of paternalism, intensifying scrutiny of the bilateral relationship.

Kenya has rapidly expanded defence pacts with multiple nations, including **China**, **Zimbabwe**, the **Czech Republic**, **Ethiopia**, and now France. This diversification is praised for bolstering national security but raises alarms about potential foreign interference in Kenya's sovereignty, economy, and politics.

### Defence Cooperation Agreement

A cornerstone of the partnership is the April 2026 defence cooperation agreement, which followed the deployment of **800 French troops** to Mombasa for joint exercises with the **Kenya Defence Forces (KDF)**. The pact, renewable every five years, covers maritime security, intelligence sharing, peacekeeping, and humanitarian aid or disaster response. France has since stationed **600 military personnel** in Kenya to train KDF forces.

Critics highlight provisions granting French troops diplomatic-style immunity, routing disputes through diplomatic channels instead of Kenyan courts, and allowing convicted personnel to serve sentences in France. Paris holds primary jurisdiction over offences by its soldiers on Kenyan soil. These terms, opponents claim, prioritize French strategic interests in the Western Indian Ocean—a vital area for global shipping, military operations, and counterterrorism—over Kenyan autonomy.

Kenyan politician **Nelson Koech**, chair of the National Assembly's Departmental Committee on Defence, Intelligence, and Foreign Relations, refuted immunity claims in an interview. "It is completely untrue… we have said crimes like murder must be tried in Kenya," he stated. Koech emphasized France's broader support through various projects and argued that past tensions with West Africa should not preclude beneficial ties. He clarified that the agreement does not imply a permanent French presence, though parliamentary sources indicated France is advocating for one.

This defence deal echoes concerns from France's West African engagements, where similar arrangements have been seen as tools for maintaining influence. While Koech downplays risks, the source notes a potential contradiction: official denials versus insider reports on troop permanency pushes.

### Economic and Strategic Gains

Beyond security, the partnership offers tangible economic upsides. France is Kenya's fourth-largest source of foreign direct investment, with **1.8 billion euros ($2.1 billion)** committed over the past decade. Kenya consumes more French products than any other East African nation, and the number of French companies operating there has surged to **at least 140** in 2026, from **40** in 2013.

In March 2026, **Business France East Africa**, alongside the French Chamber of Commerce in Kenya, released a business guide to ease French investments. This reflects growing French interest in Kenya's market, providing Paris with economic opportunities, political stability, and access to the Indian Ocean. For Kenya, benefits include infrastructure development, enhanced security, and elevated global standing.

Yet, these rewards come with risks. Rapid ratification of multiple foreign agreements could dilute sovereignty, as critics warn of neo-colonial dynamics interfering in domestic affairs.

### What This Means for Travelers

For visitors traveling between France and Kenya—or using Kenya as a gateway to East Africa—this partnership could streamline security and business travel. Enhanced maritime and intelligence cooperation might improve safety on Indian Ocean routes, benefiting cruise lines and ferries linking Mombasa to French territories like Réunion Island. French investments could spur infrastructure upgrades at Nairobi's Jomo Kenyatta International Airport, easing connections for Europeans heading to safari destinations.

However, sovereignty concerns might indirectly affect visa policies or diplomatic relations. Travelers should monitor updates via official channels, such as [France's practical info for Kenya](https://go2-france.com/practical-info/). For those planning trips involving French-Kenyan business events, the summit highlights Kenya's rising profile, potentially increasing flight options from Paris to Nairobi. Safety remains strong in Kenya's stable regions, but coastal areas like Mombasa warrant caution due to military activities.

If tensions escalate over perceived neo-colonialism, it could impact perceptions of France in Africa, influencing cultural exchanges or tourism promotions.

### What's Next

The Africa Forward 2026 summit will likely solidify these ties, with outcomes on trade and security shaping future deals. Kenya's multi-nation pacts suggest a balanced foreign policy, but parliamentary debates on the defence agreement's fine print could lead to amendments. France may push harder for a permanent base, testing Koech's assurances.

Observers will watch for economic spillovers, like more French firms entering Kenyan tourism, potentially creating jobs and attractions. For France, maintaining this foothold is crucial amid African realignments. Broader implications include stronger EU-Africa links, with Kenya as a model. Travelers and investors should stay informed, as evolving dynamics could open new opportunities or introduce uncertainties in this key bilateral relationship.
