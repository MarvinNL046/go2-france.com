# Le Pen Pledges to Withdraw France from NATO Command if Elected

Far-right political leader **Marine Le Pen** announced on Friday that she would withdraw France from NATO's integrated military command structure should she be elected president, according to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/le-pen-says-france-should-quit-nato-command-structure-2026-05-22/). The statement represents a major foreign policy position that could reshape France's relationship with the Western military alliance if her party gains power in upcoming elections.

## Background on France and NATO

France's relationship with NATO has been complex and distinctive since the alliance's founding. Under former President **Charles de Gaulle**, France withdrew from NATO's integrated command structure in 1966, though it remained a member of the alliance itself. This arrangement allowed France to maintain military independence while benefiting from collective defense guarantees.

In 2009, President **Nicolas Sarkozy** reversed this decision, reintegrating France fully into NATO's command structure. This move was intended to strengthen France's voice within the alliance and improve coordination with other member states. Since then, France has participated actively in NATO operations and maintained its integrated status.

Le Pen's proposal would essentially reverse Sarkozy's 2009 decision, returning France to a status similar to what existed during the Gaullist era—a NATO member outside the integrated command framework.

## What Le Pen's Position Means

Le Pen's statement reflects the far-right National Rally party's broader skepticism toward international institutions and emphasis on national sovereignty. The proposal appeals to voters who prioritize French independence in military and defense matters over deeper integration with Western allies.

A withdrawal from NATO's integrated command would allow France to:
- Maintain greater autonomy over its military operations
- Reduce coordination obligations with other NATO members
- Potentially reduce financial contributions to joint NATO structures

However, such a move would also complicate France's defense relationships with neighboring European countries and the United States, and could weaken France's influence within NATO decision-making processes.

## Implications for France and Europe

The proposal carries significant geopolitical weight at a time when European security concerns have intensified. France's current integrated role within NATO is central to European defense coordination, particularly given its military capabilities and permanent seat on the UN Security Council.

A withdrawal from the integrated command structure would not automatically remove France from NATO itself—membership in the alliance and participation in its command structure are separate matters. Nevertheless, such a move would signal a fundamental shift in France's approach to European security and transatlantic relations.

The timing of Le Pen's announcement comes as France continues to navigate its role as a leading European power while managing relationships with both the United States and other EU member states.

## What's Next

Le Pen's statement is part of her broader political platform ahead of potential presidential elections. Whether this policy would be implemented depends on electoral outcomes and the political landscape at the time of any future presidency.

For now, France remains fully integrated into NATO's command structure under its current government. Any change to this status would require a significant political shift and would likely face scrutiny from France's European partners and NATO allies.
